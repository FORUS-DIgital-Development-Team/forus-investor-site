INVESTMENT CASE · THE ECONOMICS OF GROWTH

Capital already invested.
Value still to unlock.

Management’s case for FORUS is straightforward: the core platform has been funded and built, running costs are very low, and growing usage has the potential to produce substantial cash over that operating base.

WHY FORUS

Three reasons to examine the opportunity.

01

An existing platform asset

The core development investment has already been made. Commercial expansion can build on that asset, with further spending directed to deployment, integration and growth.

02

A lean operating structure

Shared technology can support multiple networks. A low fixed-cost base creates the potential for revenue to grow faster than overhead as activity scales.

03

A path to recurring cash

Useful services can create repeat transactions and retained revenue. After delivery costs, operating expenses and cash requirements, that activity can contribute to operating cash generation.

FOLLOW THE CASH LOGIC

How platform activity can create investor value.

  1. 01

    Repeat usage

    Participants return to services that solve everyday needs.

  2. 02

    Retained revenue

    Agreed fees and allocations determine what the relevant company earns.

  3. 03

    Operating cash

    Collections exceed the cash costs of running and serving the business.

  4. 04

    Investor value

    Cash can support reinvestment, debt service and potential shareholder distributions.

Cash generation depends on adoption, pricing, collections and costs. Revenue, EBITDA, operating cash and distributable cash are distinct; taxes, capital spending, financing and distribution decisions also affect investor outcomes.

ILLUSTRATIVE TERMS

How one earn-out could work.

Primary capital; no assumed secondary proceeds.

1

Initial investment

USD 30m into the rights-holding investee for 15% fully diluted equity: USD 170m pre-money and USD 200m post-money in this example. Share class, entity and protections are open.

2

Adoption earn-back

At 5m, 25m and 65m verified paying active users, 2, 2 and 1 percentage points respectively transfer from the investor stake to a founder/team pool. The investor retains 13%, 11% or 10% as gates are met.

3

Exit participation

At a hypothetical USD 20bn equity exit, an undiluted 10% investor stake yields USD 2bn, or 66.7× the initial USD 30m. Remaining holders share USD 18bn according to their actual cap table.

This is a suggested negotiation framework, not the August 2026 transaction. Milestones, vesting window, share-transfer mechanics, change-of-control treatment, governance and tax must be agreed.

THE USD 20BN GAP

The historical unit assumption alone does not support that exit.

The August model assumed USD 1.75 annual N.V. revenue retained per active user. If every one of the pipeline's 65m full-potential users became paying active users, annual retained revenue would be USD 113.75m. At an illustrative 20× revenue multiple, that is USD 2.275bn of equity value, before accounting for costs, debt or valuation differences.

A USD 20bn equity value at 20× would require USD 1bn annual retained revenue: about 571m paying active users at USD 1.75 each, or USD 15.38 retained per user across 65m. That requires additional monetisation, substantially more adoption, a different multiple or a combination. None has been demonstrated.
